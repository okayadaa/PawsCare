# Wound & Injury After-Care Instructions

## Wound Care Basics

- Check the wound **twice daily** for changes.
- Keep the area **clean and dry**.
- Prevent licking, biting, and scratching. An e-collar ("cone"), recovery suit, or t-shirt can help, depending on where the wound is.
- Only clean the wound the way your vet showed you. Warm water or a mild salt-water rinse is common.
- **Don't** use hydrogen peroxide, rubbing alcohol, soap, shampoo, tea tree oil, or creams unless your vet tells you to. Some slow healing, and some are harmful if licked.

**Normal healing:** mild redness, slight swelling, and a scab forming, with pain decreasing over time.

## Bandage Care (if applied)

- Keep the bandage **clean and dry**. Cover it with a plastic bag or bootie for outdoor bathroom breaks.
- Check the bandage at least twice a day. Make sure it hasn't slipped and isn't too tight or too loose.
- If the toes show below the bandage, check them for swelling, redness, a bad smell, or feeling hot or cold.
- Don't remove the bandage yourself unless your vet tells you to.

**Call the vet if the bandage:** gets wet or dirty, smells bad, is soaked with blood or discharge, slips, feels too tight or too loose, causes swelling or color changes above or below it, or your pet starts chewing it.

## Activity

- Restrict running, jumping, and rough play until healed.
- Leash walks for bathroom breaks only for dogs; confine cats and small pets indoors.
- No baths or swimming until the vet approves.

## Medication

- Give antibiotics and pain medication exactly as prescribed, and finish antibiotics unless your vet says to stop.
- **Never** give human pain relievers. They can be toxic to pets, and acetaminophen is fatal to cats.

## Call the Vet If You See

- Fresh bleeding, or discharge that is thick, green, yellow, or bloody for several days
- Increasing swelling, redness, or heat
- Foul odor from the wound
- The wound opening or stitches coming loose
- Lethargy or refusal to eat
- Signs of pain (whining, hiding, guarding the area)
