# Spay / Neuter After-Care Instructions

## First 24 Hours

- Keep your pet **warm, quiet, and confined** to a small space, away from other pets and children.
- Grogginess, wobbliness, and low energy from anesthesia are normal. Some pets whine or cry the first night.
- In the evening, offer a small amount of water and a small amount of their regular food.
- Appetite can take up to 48 hours to return to normal.
- Supervise around stairs and furniture.

## Activity Restrictions (usually 10–14 days)

- No running, jumping, or rough play. Follow your vet's timeline.
- Dogs: short leash walks for bathroom breaks only.
- Cats & small pets: keep confined indoors; avoid high perches and climbing.
- No baths, swimming, or playing in rain or deep snow until your vet says the incision has healed.
- Males can still get an unspayed female pregnant for up to a month after surgery.

## Incision Care

- Check the incision **twice a day** until it has healed.
- Prevent licking or chewing. Keep the e-collar ("cone") or a recovery suit on for as long as your vet says, usually 10–14 days. A cone should reach about two inches past your pet's nose.
- Keep the area clean and dry; do not apply any ointment unless prescribed.

**Normal in the first few days:** slight redness, minor swelling, small bruising, or a tiny amount of bloody seepage. These should improve each day.

**Call the vet if you see:** significant swelling, colored discharge (yellow, green, or pus), a foul smell, a gap in the incision, or redness and swelling that get worse instead of better.

## Pain & Medication

- Give all medication exactly as prescribed.
- **Never** give human pain relievers (ibuprofen, naproxen, acetaminophen, aspirin). They can cause stomach ulcers, kidney failure, or liver failure, and acetaminophen is fatal to cats.

## Call the Vet Immediately If

- Your pet is still not eating or drinking by the third day after surgery
- Vomiting keeps happening, or doesn't stop (go to an emergency clinic if it won't stop)
- Pale gums
- Bathroom habits haven't returned to normal within a few days
- Signs of severe pain or lethargy

**Go to an emergency clinic right away for:** bleeding that doesn't stop, difficulty breathing, not waking up or not being able to stand, or an incision that has opened completely.
