# Dental Cleaning & Extraction After-Care Instructions

## First 24 Hours

- Expect grogginess and mild wobbliness from anesthesia. It usually wears off within a few hours but can last overnight.
- Keep your pet calm and in a quiet, comfortable space.
- Offer water once fully awake and alert.
- Your pet may be reluctant to eat or chew for the first day.

## Eating & Diet

- If no teeth were removed, feed the usual amount of food.
- After extractions, feed **soft food only** for as long as your vet says, usually **at least 10 days**. Canned food, or dry kibble softened with warm water, works well.
- **Avoid** dry kibble, hard treats, bones, rawhide, chew toys, rope toys, and tug-of-war while the mouth heals. They can damage the stitches.

## Mouth Care

- Do **not** brush teeth, look in the mouth, or touch the extraction sites until your vet says they have healed.
- A small amount of blood-tinged saliva on the first day can be normal.
- Most mild issues settle within 48 to 72 hours.

**Call the vet if you see:** bleeding from the mouth, swelling that gets worse, refusal to eat after 24 hours, pawing or rubbing at the mouth, torn stitches, or pain that continues despite medication.

## Medication

- Give antibiotics and pain medication exactly as prescribed, **even if your pet seems fine**. Pets often hide pain.
- **Never** give human pain relievers. They can be toxic to pets, and acetaminophen is fatal to cats.

## Activity

- Keep activity calm for the first 24 hours.
- Resume the normal routine once your pet is alert and eating comfortably.
- Call your vet if your pet is still very drowsy 24 hours after the procedure.
- Many clinics schedule a follow-up check about 10–12 days after extractions.
